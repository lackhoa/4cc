Contour extraction from a patch

How a contour (the line where the surface turns away from the camera) is computed from a Coons patch, and where it goes wrong. One shared camera: drag empty space in any view to orbit, wheel to zoom.

1 · What a contour is

At every surface point compute f = n · v: the unit normal dotted with the direction to the eye. f > 0 faces the camera (blue), f < 0 faces away (red). The contour is the set f = 0, drawn here in yellow from a dense 96×96 evaluation, the "ground truth" the later stages approximate. Orbit and watch it slide: it's a property of the camera, not of the patch.

n(u,v) = normalize(∂S/∂u × ∂S/∂v)    v(u,v) = normalize(eye − S(u,v))    f = n · v
the partials come from the Coons formula directly; here they're finite differences on S, which is enough

2 · Sampling on the grid: marching squares

The tablet tessellates a patch on a 16×16 grid. Evaluate f at the grid corners (positive, negative). Every grid edge whose two ends differ in sign has a crossing somewhere on it. Place the crossing, then inside each cell join its crossings in pairs: that's a contour segment. This is marching squares on the scalar field f.

Where on the edge? Linear interpolation of f is the cheap answer: s = f₀ / (f₀ − f₁). But the segment then runs straight across the cell and the yellow reference bends: at 16×16 the result is visibly jaggy. The fix is cheap because the surface is analytic: bisect along the edge in (u,v) space, evaluating the true f each time, and the crossing lands on the real contour. Compare with the toggle at low grid counts.

Root-finding fixes where the crossing sits on each edge, not the straight segment between two crossings. A cell that the contour bends through still gets one straight segment, so the grid count still matters, just much less. Also: a cell with 4 crossings (the contour passes through twice) is ambiguous; it's paired arbitrarily here.

3 · Joining segments into polylines

Segments come out per cell, unordered. Two segments meet exactly when they share a grid edge, so key each crossing by its edge index and walk: start at any unused segment, follow the shared edges until the chain closes or ends at the patch boundary. Each polyline gets its own color below. A contour that leaves through the patch boundary continues on the neighbouring patch; joining those is the same walk keyed by the shared boundary stroke instead of a grid edge.

The 3-sided trap. The tablet's 3-sided patch is a Coons patch with one side collapsed to a point. At that corner both partials shrink to zero, the normal is undefined and f is noise: sign flips everywhere, spurious crossings, garbage segments. Toggle the collapse and watch the corner. The remedy is to skip cells whose un-normalized normal is shorter than a threshold (the mask toggle); the contour then ends a cell short of the corner, which is what an artist's line would do anyway.

4 · Coarse zeros, then fit a curve

Instead of chasing accuracy with a finer grid or root-finding, keep the grid coarse and fit a smooth curve through the crossings. The trick that makes a coarse fit good: the contour's tangent at each crossing is free. In (u,v) the gradient of f is perpendicular to the contour, so the tangent is the gradient rotated 90° (two extra evaluations of f per crossing); map it to 3D with the surface partials. Then every cell segment becomes one cubic Hermite through its two crossings with those tangents, i.e. a bezier chain, the same object as a drawn stroke.

∇f = (∂f/∂u, ∂f/∂v)    tangent_uv = (−∂f/∂v, ∂f/∂u)    T = ∂S/∂u · tangent_u + ∂S/∂v · tangent_v

Below: grey chords are stage 3's polyline, teal is the bezier chain through the same crossings, yellow the dense reference. Drop the grid to 4 or 5: the chords are a polygon, the chain still hugs the reference. What the fit can't fix is a crossing that sits off the true curve (visible without root-find at very low grid counts); that error shrinks with the square of the cell size.

Sign checks miss an edge the contour crosses twice. Along one grid edge f is a polynomial (degree 8 with a perspective eye), so exact root isolation is possible there if a contour ever visibly breaks; not done here.

5 · From polyline to contour stroke

A polyline is only a contour. A contour stroke is a contour with style: taper and color. Once the polyline exists it goes down the same path as any stroke: a camera-facing ribbon whose radius follows a taper. The taper here runs over the polyline's own arc length, because a contour that ends mid-patch (it left through a seam or turned back) has no stroke end to inherit from. Color and base radius would come from blending the patch's boundary strokes at the contour point's (u,v); here it's one fixed color.

Not shown: occlusion. In the app the ribbon is depth-tested against the patches with a small bias, exactly like drawn strokes, so the far half of a closed contour hides behind the patch.

6 · Where the style comes from: Coons-blend the boundary strokes

The patch's four boundary strokes each carry a style (here: a color and a base radius; in the desktop app, radii and lightness_additions). A contour point lives at some (u,v) on the patch, and the patch already knows how to blend four sides into an interior: the same Coons formula that blends positions blends style. Each side's style is constant along it, so the formula collapses to: interpolate bottom and top in v, add left and right interpolated in u, subtract the bilinear blend of the corner values (a corner belongs to two strokes; take their mean).

style(u,v) = (1−v)·bottom + v·top + (1−u)·left + u·right − [(1−u)(1−v)·c00 + u(1−v)·c10 + (1−u)v·c01 + uv·c11]

Below, the four sides are painted in their own style (bottom thick, top thin, left, right). The contour ribbon picks up whichever side it runs closest to and fades between them. A contour near the jawline stroke looks like the jawline stroke. No nearest-stroke search, nothing jumps when the camera moves: the blend is continuous over the patch.

Stage 5's arc-length taper still applies on top of the blended radius, so a contour ending mid-patch still fades out. Prerequisite in the tablet: strokes have no style yet; until they do, every side blends to the one fixed style and this stage is a no-op.
