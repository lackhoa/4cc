The face thirds

Loomis's next step after the brow line (see loomis-head, step 4): the face hangs below the ball in three equal units, brow to hairline, brow to nose base, nose base to chin. The book gives the unit twice: half a radius on Plate 1, two thirds on Plate 18. This page measures the two lower units on the skull and its mandible, from the brow line of skull-brow-line down to the nasal spine and the menton. The hairline is not on bone, so the upper unit is not tested. All lengths in mm, in the Frankfurt frame. main menu

Which plate does the skull agree with?

  • Two units, two answers. Plate 1 hangs the face half a radius per unit off the ball; Plate 18 makes the whole head 3.5 units tall with the ball 1.5 units, i.e. two thirds of a radius per unit. They differ by a third of a radius over the two lower units, about 30 mm on this ball, more than any picking error.
  • Bone is not skin. The nasal spine is a few mm behind and under the soft nose base, the menton a few mm above the soft chin. Both errors push the measured units short of what the book (drawn on faces) would give; a couple of mm, not 30.
  • The teeth. The mouth is closed in this scan (the condyles sit in the joint), so the chin height is the closed-mouth one, which is what the book draws.

Glossary

brow line
The ball's horizontal section at the height of its center; Loomis's eyebrow line. Measured on the skull on the skull-brow-line page.
nasal spine
The anterior nasal spine, the bony point on the midline under the nose opening, where the nose base (the septum) attaches. Loomis's nose line. Picked by hand on this page, stored in the skull's landmarks file as nasal_spine.
menton
The lowest point of the chin on the midline of the mandible. Loomis's chin line. Picked by hand on this page, stored in the mandible's landmarks file as menton.
unit
One third of the face in the book: brow to hairline = brow to nose base = nose base to chin. 0.5 r on Plate 1, 2/3 r on Plate 18.
