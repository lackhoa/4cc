The eyes

"The face is five eyes wide, one eye between the eyes" (Loomis, Finch) is about the eye you see: the lid opening. The bone only has the orbit, the socket, which is far bigger than that. What the bone does fix is the eye's center: the eyeball sits in the middle of the socket, so the midpoint of the socket's two corners is where the eye is. Five eyes across the face put the two centers at 1.5 and 3.5 eye widths, two fifths of the face width apart; that, and the height of the centers against the brow and nose lines, is what this page measures. What the skull says

  • The socket is not the eye. The lid opening is narrower than the rim by a lid's worth on each side, and that is soft tissue this skull does not have. So the orbit's width and the gap between the orbits get no verdict here; the rule's "one eye" cannot be checked on bone. TODO quote the adult orbit width vs lid-opening width from the literature, with a source.
  • The center is bone. The eyeball sits in the middle of the socket, so the socket's midpoint is the eye's center whatever the lids do. Five eyes across the face is a statement about the centers: two fifths of the face width apart. That is the one width number the rule and the skull can be compared on.
  • The eye line. Loomis hangs the eyes just under the brow line; Finch puts them halfway between the brow and nose lines. The centers' height gives both a number.
  • One skull. One data point (male, see the models' README); eye spacing varies a lot between people.

Glossary

brow line
The ball's horizontal section at the height of its center; Loomis's eyebrow line. Measured on the skull on the skull-brow-line page.
orbit
The eye socket. Its inner corner is the dacryon, where the rim meets the nose bridge; its outer corner is the frontozygomatic suture, where the brow bone meets the cheekbone on the outer rim. Both picked by hand on this page on one side, mirrored, stored in the skull's landmarks file as orbit_inner_corner and orbit_outer_corner.
eye center
The midpoint of the two corners (orbit_center on the page, not stored). Stands in for the eyeball's center.
nose line
The nasal spine's height, as on skull-face-thirds, from the landmarks file.
side plane
Loomis's flat on the side of the ball: the mirrored planes at the vault's widest point, from skull-side-cuts (fit_side_planes_mirrored).
