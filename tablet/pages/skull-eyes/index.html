The eyes

"The face is five eyes wide, one eye between the eyes" (Loomis, Finch) is about the eye you see: the lid opening. The bone only has the orbit, the socket, which is far bigger than that. What the rules are about is the eye's center, and the scan has the eyeball itself: this page fits a sphere to the right globe of the eyeball mesh and calls its center the eye center (the left is the mirror). The midpoint of the socket's two corners is kept as a comparison: it sits well above the globe. Five eyes across the face put the two centers at 1.5 and 3.5 eye widths, two fifths of the face width apart; that, and the height of the centers against the brow and nose lines, is what this page measures. What the skull says

  • The socket is not the eye. The lid opening is narrower than the rim by a lid's worth on each side, and that is soft tissue this skull does not have. So the orbit's width and the gap between the orbits get no verdict here; the rule's "one eye" cannot be checked on bone. TODO quote the adult orbit width vs lid-opening width from the literature, with a source.
  • The center is the globe, measured. The eyeball mesh gives the center directly, and it is not the corners' midpoint: the rim's widest points (picked at the nasion's height) sit well above the globe, so a midpoint of rim points overshoots. Five eyes across the face is a statement about the centers: two fifths of the face width apart. Against the cheekbone width the centers miss; against the side-plane width they hit the two fifths, which is the width Loomis's ball actually has at the eyes.
  • The eye line. Loomis hangs the eyes just under the brow line; the globe's height gives that a number, and it is a good way under, not just under. (Finch gives no eye height in the video; an earlier version of this page claimed "halfway", which was a mix-up with his mouth rule.)
  • One skull. One data point (male, see the models' README); eye spacing varies a lot between people.

Glossary

brow line
The ball's horizontal section at the height of its center; Loomis's eyebrow line. Measured on the skull on the skull-brow-line page.
nasion
The top of the nose bridge on the midline, where the nasal bones meet the brow bone; the dip under the glabella. Picked on this page, stored as nasion.
orbit
The eye socket. Its inner corner is the dacryon, where the rim meets the nose bridge, picked as the inner rim at the nasion's height (the rim's innermost point); its outer corner is the frontozygomatic suture, where the brow bone meets the cheekbone on the outer rim, picked as the outer rim's furthest-out point, also at the nasion's height (Khoa's pick, 2026-09-26: both corners level with the nasion). Both picked by hand on this page on one side, mirrored, stored in the skull's landmarks file as orbit_inner_corner and orbit_outer_corner.
eye center
The center of the sphere fitted (fit_sphere_algebraic) to the right globe of the Z-Anatomy eyeball mesh, eyeball_center on the page; the left eye is its mirror. Not stored: it is computed from the mesh every load.
corner midpoint
The midpoint of the two corners (orbit_center on the page, not stored). Kept as a comparison against the eye center; it sits above the globe because the rim's widest points do.
nose line
The nasal spine's height, as on skull-face-thirds, from the landmarks file.
side plane
Loomis's flat on the side of the ball: the mirrored planes at the vault's widest point, from skull-side-cuts (fit_side_planes_mirrored).
