The mouth line and the jaw angle

Two lines below the nose that skull-face-thirds leaves out. Loomis draws them without numbers; David Finch's walk-through of the method says the mouth sits "just about halfway" from the nose base to the chin, "a little higher" on an idealized face, and that the back of the jaw is level with the mouth. On bone the mouth is the teeth line (the upper incisor tip; the upper teeth are a third mesh, the skull mesh stops at the alveolar edge) and the back of the jaw is the gonion, the mandible's angle. What the skull says

  • The teeth line vs the lip line. The mouth line an artist draws is where the lips meet, which on a closed mouth is at the upper incisor edge or a little below it; the teeth line is the closest thing on bone. The lower incisor tip is printed as a check that the mouth is closed in this scan (it should sit a few mm behind and above the upper tip, an overbite).
  • The gonion is off the midline. It is the corner of the mandible on the side of the face, so it lives in the side view, not the section; in the section it is drawn at its height and depth as if projected onto the midline plane.
  • One skull. A jaw angle's height varies a lot between people (long-faced vs square-jawed); this is one data point against a rule of thumb.

Glossary

brow line
The ball's horizontal section at the height of its center; Loomis's eyebrow line. Measured on the skull on the skull-brow-line page.
teeth line
The height of the upper incisor tip: where the upper and lower teeth meet with the mouth closed, the bony stand-in for the mouth line. From incisor_upper in the upper teeth's landmarks file.
gonion
The angle of the mandible: the corner at the back of the jaw where the horizontal body turns up into the ramus. Picked by hand on this page on one side, mirrored to the other, stored in the mandible's landmarks file as gonion.
nose base, chin
The nasal spine and the menton, as on skull-face-thirds, from the landmarks files.
