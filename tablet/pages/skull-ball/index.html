Where is the ball in a real skull?

The Loomis head starts from a ball (see loomis-head). Three ways to fit that ball to the Z-Anatomy skull, side by side, with the misses shown as a heat map. All lengths in mm, in the Frankfurt frame. main menu

Three candidates

The heat map colors every skull vertex by its distance to the candidate's surface, but each fit and its numbers only use the vertices above that candidate's cut plane (the vault). The face is colored anyway so you can see how far off the ball is where Loomis hangs the face.

The glabella

Candidates b and c cut the skull along the plane through the two porions and the glabella, so the fit sees only the brain case. The glabella is a point on the midline profile, so pick it there: the tracing below is the skull cut by the midsagittal plane (grey), with the front silhouette highlighted. Drag the marker up or down; it stays on the silhouette. Small ticks mark the bumps and dips of the silhouette. The glabella sits level with the eyebrows, so the page draws the orbit rim (the top of the eye socket, dotted line) as a ruler; the nasion is the dip just under it, and everything bumping out below that is the nasal bones, which on this mesh kink outward well under the brows. The page does not guess the point: the marker comes from the landmarks file, or starts at eyebrow height until you pick.

What each one gets right and wrong

  • a. Frankfurt sphere. Everything above the Frankfurt plane, which includes the brow ridge, the orbit rims and the upper face. Those pull the center forward and down, and the sphere ends up too small for the brain case: the forehead and the back of the skull stick out of it (red in profile) while the sides sit close to it. It is the fit the C++ app made first; the table row reproduces it.
  • b. cranium sphere. Same shape, tighter set: only the brain case above the porion–glabella plane. The center moves up and back and the misses shrink, but a sphere is still too round: the cranium is longer front to back than it is wide, so the skull still sticks out at the forehead and the back while the sides fit. This is Loomis's ball: a sphere with slices cut off the sides.
  • c. cranium ellipsoid. Axes along side / up / front, same vertex set as b. The three semi-axes say directly how much of an egg the cranium is. Best numbers, hardest to draw: an ellipsoid has no single radius to measure other lengths against, which is the whole point of the ball.

Glossary

porion (left / right)
The top of the ear hole. Two landmarks on the mesh; their midpoint is the origin of the frame.
orbitale
The lowest point of the eye socket rim. One landmark.
glabella
The most forward point of the forehead between the brows, on the midline. Picked on this page.
Frankfurt frame
The plane through the two porions and the orbitale is anatomy's "head held level" reference. Frame axes: side = left porion to right porion, up = the plane's normal, front = side × up; origin = porion middle.
vault
The skull vertices a fit is allowed to see: above the Frankfurt plane for candidate a, above the porion–glabella plane for b and c, and only the outer surface (the mesh is a closed shell; the inner table is a second surface 5–10 mm inside the one we draw and would drag every fit inward).
rms
Root mean square of the residuals over the vault: the typical miss in mm.
