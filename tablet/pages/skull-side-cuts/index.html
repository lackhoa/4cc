Slicing the sides off the ball

Loomis cuts "a fairly thin slice" off each side of the ball (see loomis-head, step 2). This page measures that slice on the Z-Anatomy skull: the cranium sphere from skull-ball, then two mirrored planes fitted to the flat sides. All lengths in mm, in the Frankfurt frame. main menu

The fit rule

A flat is where the skull sits well inside the sphere. Take every vault vertex whose sphere residual is below −threshold, and let half_width be their mean distance from the midline (the least-squares plane parallel to the midline through them). Both sides get the same number. The threshold defaults to the sphere's own rms, i.e. "inside by more than a typical miss"; move it to see how stable the answer is.

What the cuts buy

  • a. cranium sphere. The ball from skull-ball, candidate b there: the sides of the skull sit inside it (blue), the forehead and the back stick out (red). A sphere is too wide for a cranium.
  • b. sphere with side cuts. Same sphere, the two flats clipped off. The blue on the sides should go pale; the red at the front and back stays, since the cuts do nothing there. The rms over the vault says how much of the sphere's miss was the sides. What is left is the egg (longer than wide), which no side cut fixes.

Glossary

vault
The skull vertices the fits see: above the plane through the two porions and the glabella (the cranium cut), same as skull-ball's candidates b and c.
flat
A vault vertex sitting inside the sphere by more than the threshold. The flats are the temporal sides of the cranium, the part Loomis slices off.
half_width
Distance from the midline to each cut plane, mm. In Loomis's terms the slice depth is radius − half_width.
rim
The circle where a cut plane meets the sphere: the edge of Loomis's side plane, whose vertical center line carries the ear.
